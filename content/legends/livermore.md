## Signature ideas (paraphrased from his own writing)
- **The pivotal point.** He made money when he had the patience to wait for the market to reach a pivotal point
  before trading; then he sat tight and let the move run. If a stock does not act right after crossing its pivotal
  point, that is a danger signal. (*How to Trade in Stocks*, ch. V "The Pivotal Point".)
- **Never average losses.** Don't make a second trade if the first shows a loss; add only to a position that is
  working. (*How to Trade in Stocks*, ch. II and IV.)
- **Bank part of the wins.** After a big success, draw out half of the profit and put it aside. (*How to Trade in
  Stocks*, ch. IV "Money in the Hand".)
- **Keep your own records** and do your own thinking. (*How to Trade in Stocks*, ch. I.)
- **Sitting tight.** In *Reminiscences*, the narrator says the big money came from his sitting, not his thinking;
  being right is common, being right and sitting tight is rare. Also: never argue with the tape. (Lefèvre, 1923.)

## Contested / handle with care
- Dollar figures for single trades (1907, 1929) come from press and biographies; we don't put them on the card.
- *Reminiscences* is semi-fictional: we attribute its lines to the book, not as Livermore's own words.
- His death was a suicide; we don't use it for hooks or jokes.
