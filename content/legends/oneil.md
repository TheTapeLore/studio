## The mistake, and its cause (used in Lore 010)
- The O'Neil Fund (launched 1965) rose 116% in 1967, that year's best mutual fund per FundScope. (WealthManagement.com)
- It suffered major outflows when the market turned down in the late 1960s; by its 1975 sale, assets had shrunk to
  $6 million from a $49 million peak. (Wikipedia, citing the WSJ obituary; WealthManagement.com, citing the LA Times)
- His own explanation to the Los Angeles Times: they bought too many small companies and were growing too fast.
  (Wikipedia, citing the WSJ obituary.) We paraphrase it on screen and turn it into the rule "own a few stocks, and
  know each one well".

## Why he's a legend (used in Lore 010)
1. He studied stocks by computer before almost anyone (the first computerized daily stock database).
2. He measured what more than 1,000 big winners had in common before their big runs, back to the 1880s.
3. He gave that research to everyday investors in a daily paper.
