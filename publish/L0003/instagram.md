## Caption

You made $500. Proud? Depends what you risked. On $100 risk that's +5R. On $2,000 it's +0.25R.

R is what you lose if your stop gets hit. You set it before you buy, then you measure everything with it.

How to get 1R:
1R = (entry − stop) × shares
Entry $50, stop $48, 50 shares: 1R = $100
Price gets to $58: that's +4R
Stopped out instead: −1R

We counted 20 simulated trades in R: every loser was −1R, the total was +3.5R. That's $350 if 1R is $100, or $35,000 if 1R is $10,000. R doesn't care which account it is.

If a loss ever shows up as −3R, your stop was more of a suggestion. Or price gapped past it. Either way, find out why.
