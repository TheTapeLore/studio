## Caption

Jesse Livermore made $1 million in one day in 1907. He also went bankrupt more than once. Both halves are worth learning.

Why traders still call him a legend:
1. He taught himself to read markets from prices alone, what we now call technical analysis.
2. He made fortunes in the two great crashes of his time, 1907 and 1929 (about $100 million, reportedly).
3. In 1907, with the market at his mercy, J.P. Morgan asked him to stop selling. He stopped.
4. A century later, his story, Reminiscences of a Stock Operator, is still read by traders.

How he won: he waited for the pivotal point, a price that starts the move. He bought when price broke through it, added more only while the trade kept working, then sat tight.

How he lost: he broke his own rules. In 1908 he followed a friend's cotton tips instead of the market, and kept buying as cotton fell. He lost most of his fortune. He also lost every cent he put into businesses outside trading. He filed for bankruptcy in 1915 and again in 1934.

The rules he wrote down in 1940, paid for in losses:
1. Wait for the pivotal point. Don't jump in early.
2. Add only to a trade that's working. Never to a loser.
3. Trust your own rules over anyone's tips.
4. Take some profit out after a big win.

Learning them here costs less. Save this one.

Sources: How to Trade in Stocks (Livermore, 1940); Reminiscences of a Stock Operator (Lefèvre, 1923); Wikipedia biography.

Education only. Not investment advice. Historical charts; past results don't predict future ones.
