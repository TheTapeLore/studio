## Title

Position sizing formula: wider stop, smaller size | Lore 005

## Description

Always buy 100 shares? With a $4 stop, that's $400 at risk instead of $100.
Pick the risk first, then let the stop pick the size: shares = risk ÷ stop distance.

On a $10,000 account risking 1% ($100): a $1 stop is 100 shares, $2 is 50, $4 is 25. Round down. The risk stays $100 wherever the stop goes. Full size on a wide stop is the scary one.
