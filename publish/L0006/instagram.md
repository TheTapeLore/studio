## Caption

Same 200 trades. One trader risked 0.5% a trade, the other 2%. Worst drop: −6% vs −23%.

We simulated 1,000 traders with the same edge: 40% winners, wins pay twice what losses cost. The only difference was how much of the account each trade put at risk.

What happened:
• Risk 0.5% a trade: the typical worst drop was −6%. The unluckiest 5% fell 11%.
• Risk 2% a trade: the typical worst drop was −23%. The unluckiest 5% fell 38%.
• Almost 1 in 5 runs at 2% fell more than 30% at some point.

Why? Losing streaks. Half the runs had 9 losses in a row. At 2% a trade, nine straight losses cost about 17%. At 0.5%, about 4%.

Bigger size can grow faster. It also digs deeper holes, and a −30% hole needs +43% to climb out.

You can't pick your losing streaks. You can pick your size. Pick it before the streak, not during.
