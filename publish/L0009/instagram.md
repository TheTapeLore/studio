## Caption

Cisco in April 2000: down 28% from its high, weeks after being the world's most valuable company. Bargain, right?

We followed a simple averaging-down rule on its real weekly closes: buy $10,000, then add another $10,000 every time it closed 20% lower than the last buy.

How it went:
• Five buys, $50,000 in by March 2001.
• October 2002: down 88% from the top.
• The $50,000 was worth about $17,500.
• Getting back to even needed about +185%.

The clues were there. Every bounce topped out lower than the one before, and every new buy went into a trade that was already losing. Averaging down lowers your average price, and grows the bet at the same time.

The other way: one buy, one stop 8% below it. Out in October 2000 for about $800.
