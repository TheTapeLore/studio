## Title

Averaging down Cisco: $50,000 became $17,500 | Lore 009

## Description

Cisco in April 2000 was down 28% from its high. We bought, then added $10,000 every 20% lower: $50,000 in, worth about $17,500 at the 2002 low.
One buy with a stop 8% below it would have cost about $800.

Averaging down means buying more as a stock falls. It lowers your average price and grows the bet at the same time. On Cisco's real weekly closes, every bounce topped out lower, and every new buy went into a trade already losing. Livermore and O'Neil both said the same thing: never add to a loser.
