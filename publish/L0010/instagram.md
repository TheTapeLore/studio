## Caption

William O'Neil turned $5,000 into $200,000 as a young broker. Then he studied why it worked, and kept studying for 60 years.

Why traders call him a legend:
1. He studied stocks by computer before almost anyone. His firm built the first computerized daily stock database.
2. He measured what over 1,000 big winners had in common before their big runs, back to the 1880s.
3. He gave that research to everyday investors in a daily paper, Investor's Daily (1984), now Investor's Business Daily.

His favorite setup: the cup with handle. A base is a sideways pause; the cup with handle is a rounded dip, then a smaller dip near the top. He bought when the price broke above the handle on heavy volume.

His sell rule: cut every loss 7 to 8% below your buy. No exceptions. A small loss is easy to win back. A big one isn't.

Even legends slip. His fund rose 116% in 1967, the year's best. Then stocks fell, investors pulled out, and by 1975 it had shrunk from $49 million to $6 million. His own take: they grew too fast and bought too many small stocks.

His rules, in short:
1. Buy leading stocks as they break out of a sound base.
2. Cut every loss at 7 to 8%.
3. Own a few stocks, and know each one well.
4. Follow the market's direction.
