## Caption

A record high in October 2007. Then the warnings piled up, and almost nobody was looking.

A distribution day is a day the index closes down 0.2% or more on heavier volume than the day before. The old-school read (William O'Neil's): big funds are quietly selling.

How to count:
• Count them over the last 25 trading days
• A day drops off after 25 sessions, or if the index rallies 5% above it
• O'Neil: four or five in about a month spells trouble

The S&P 500 in 2007:
• October 9: record close, count one
• Eight trading days later: five
• November 7: eight
• March 2008: down 19%. March 2009: down 57%

It's a warning, not a crystal ball. When the count piles up, go easy on new buys.
